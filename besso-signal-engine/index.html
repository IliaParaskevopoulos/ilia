What this MVP does

  1. Intake: paste any tariff notice, trade agreement text or regulation. The AI turns it into a structured event: type, stage, direction (threat / opportunity / compliance), imposing jurisdiction, affected origins, HS headings, effective date, severity and the sentence that supports it.
  2. Matching: each account has an exposure profile: the HS codes it sells, the HS codes it buys (inputs), where it produces, where its suppliers are, and where it sells. The engine matches HS headings (or whole chapters, where a measure covers a chapter) and the trade corridor, then labels each account exporter, importer, buyer of inputs or seller in market. It also applies each measure's carve-outs: the UK exemption and the 15% cap in the US pharma tariff, the exclusion of generics, and pharma's exemption from Section 301.
  3. Scoring: score = severity × HS match × corridor × ICP fit × pipeline stage × campaign window. The campaign window is open right after a story breaks (it halves every 14 days) and opens again as the next milestone approaches, such as an effective date, a signature or an expiry. That is why the polysilicon measure from August is hot again now. Tier and stage count only once they come from your CRM.
  4. Activation: for any account, the AI drafts an impact note, a campaign angle, an email and a LinkedIn post. The tone leads with help, not fear. A person edits and approves the draft before it goes to the queue.

From MVP to production

  1. Automatic ingestion every few hours from the Global Trade Alert API, WTO ePing, the Federal Register API, EUR-Lex, and a news API. Duplicates are merged and each event links to its official source.
  2. Richer exposure profiles from import/export records (Panjiva, ImportGenius) and AI classification of the accounts' product pages into HS codes.
  3. CRM sync: read accounts from Salesforce or HubSpot; write tasks, campaign members and alerts back.
  4. Learning loop: track replies and meetings for each trigger type, and re-weight the scoring.
